Interview skill. Given a described training failure, walk through Triage → Reproduce → Localize → Explain → Fix & Guard in under 5 minutes. Name the mechanism: an IEEE event (overflow, invalid op, underflow followed by division) or an optimization instability (logit growth).

1 · How non-finites are born

Overflow → ±Inf
FP16 max is 65504 (values round to Inf above 65520). x*x overflows from |x| = 256 (256² = 65536). exp(x) overflows for x > ln 65504 ≈ 11.09. Typical culprits: FP16 sum of squares or variance, an unshifted softmax, unbounded logits.
Invalid op → NaN
0/0, ∞−∞, 0·∞, √(negative), log(negative). An Inf usually turns into a NaN one op later. Example: softmax subtracts the max, so Inf − Inf = NaN.
Underflow → 0 → division
1e-8 rounds to 0 in FP16 (it is below half of 2−24). Adam's update m/(√v + eps) with v = 0 and eps = 0 gives 0/0 = NaN.
Propagation
NaN is absorbing: one NaN in a sum, matmul or all-reduce contaminates the whole result. Under DDP, one rank's NaN gradient reaches every rank through the gradient all-reduce. Also, NaN compares False to everything, so if loss > 1e4 never fires on NaN.

2 · Triage by signature

NaN at step 0–10
Bug, data or init: log(0), a masked mean over zero valid tokens, an uninitialised torch.empty buffer, missing warm-up.
Only in FP16 (fine in BF16/FP32)
Range problem: an activation above 65504, x² or exp overflow, eps underflow. Compute that op in FP32; autocast already does this for softmax and layer_norm.
GradScaler scale collapses, every step skipped
The non-finites do not depend on S. Either the forward pass or the loss is already non-finite, or backward hits an invalid op (e.g. the gradient of √ at 0). Scaling cannot fix this. Occasional skipped steps are normal: the scaler is probing upward.
Healthy for days, then a spike
Optimization instability or a bad batch. Check the max attention logit, the output log Z, the grad-norm history and the contents of the offending batch.
Slow grad-norm growth before the NaN
Divergence (LR, weight decay, logit growth), not a code bug.
Only on multi-GPU / non-deterministic
One rank's data or hardware, the communication dtype, or non-deterministic kernels. Check each rank's gradients before the all-reduce.

3 · The five-step procedure

  1. Triage. Record the step, loss curve, global grad norm, loss scale, LR, dtype and world size. Match them to a signature in §2.
  2. Reproduce. A "last good" checkpoint must contain the model, optimizer, scaler.state_dict(), LR scheduler, RNG states and data-loader position. Replay deterministically, bisect the step range and dump the offending batch. Without a reproduction, every fix is a guess.
  3. Localize. In the forward pass, use hooks to find the first module with a non-finite output (Lab 1). In backward, torch.autograd.detect_anomaly() names the forward op whose backward produced the NaN. It is slow, so use it only for debugging. verify Also check each parameter's gradient with isfinite, and set CUDA_LAUNCH_BLOCKING=1 so asynchronous CUDA errors point at the right line.
  4. Explain. Map the failure to a mechanism in §1 or §5 and show the number, e.g. "activation 312, squared = 97344 > 65504".
  5. Fix & Guard. Fix the root cause: upcast the op, put eps inside the sqrt, clamp the denominator, add qk-layernorm, lower the LR or lengthen warm-up. Then add a metric and an alert that would have caught it earlier. nan_to_num(loss) is not a fix: it hides the bug.

4 · Gotchas that separate senior candidates

  • torch.where does not block NaN gradients. The unselected branch still runs backward with a zero upstream gradient: log'(0) gives 0/0 = NaN. The fix is the "double where": sanitize the input of the risky branch (Lab 2).
  • √(Σx²) at the zero vector. The derivative of √ at 0 is ∞, and ∞·0 = NaN. Use √(Σx² + eps).
  • NaN-blind guards. Use torch.isfinite, never > thresholds.
  • Masked mean with an empty mask. sum/0 gives NaN. Use mask.sum().clamp_min(1).
  • Adam eps in FP16 is 0. Keep optimizer state in FP32 (the 16 bytes/param accounting from L1).
  • Clipping. Call clip_grad_norm_ after scaler.unscale_, and log the total norm it returns. Do not pass error_if_nonfinite=True with FP16 + GradScaler: occasional non-finite gradients are expected there and are handled by skipping the step.

5 · Instabilities that are not bugs

Based on Wortsman et al. (2023), arXiv:2309.14322.

  • verified Teams training large Transformers report instabilities that did not appear at small scale with the same hyperparameters. The authors reproduce and study them in small models trained at high learning rates.
  • verified Measuring the relationship between learning rate and loss across scales is a useful tool for identifying instabilities.
  • verified Attention logit growth appears in small models at high LR. Fig. 2 shows a 9.4M-param model at LR 0.1 and a 4.8B model at LR 0.01, with the max attention logit growing by orders of magnitude. qk-layernorm mitigates it. The paper attributes the original observation to prior work; I believe this is ViT-22B (Dehghani et al. 2023). verify
  • verify Output logit divergence: log Z drifts away from 0. It is mitigated by z-loss (about 1e-4·log²Z, from PaLM).
  • verify Warm-up, decoupled weight decay and µParam reduce LR sensitivity. As models grow wider, the gradient RMS can approach AdamW's eps, shrinking the updates; a smaller eps helps.
Whiteboard derivation: why qk-layernorm bounds logits. Non-affine LayerNorm gives each vector mean 0 and variance 1, so ‖q‖ = ‖k‖ = √d. By Cauchy–Schwarz, |q·k|/√d ≤ d/√d = √d. For d = 128 that is ≤ 11.3. Learnable gains loosen the bound, but then the gains themselves must grow, not just the weight norms.
Low-precision link (L1). In BF16 the ulp at 8192 is 213−7 = 64. Logits around 104 are quantised in steps of 64, so the softmax becomes one-hot (entropy collapse).

6 · Monitoring dashboard (the "guard" in step 5)

  • Loss, plus the global and per-layer grad norm (taken from clip_grad_norm_)
  • Loss scale and the rate of skipped steps (FP16)
  • Max |attention logit| per layer, and the mean output log Z
  • Update/weight RMS ratio per layer (healthy is roughly 1e-3; spikes come before divergence) verify
  • Activation RMS per block (residual-stream growth comes before FP16 overflow)
  • Count of non-finite values per rank, taken before the all-reduce

7 · Interview answer (STAR, aim for 60 s)

Example only. Replace it with your own incident.
S: A 1B-param FP16 fine-tune NaN'd after ~20k steps, and the loss scale collapsed to 1e-20. T: Find the root cause without restarting from scratch. A: A collapsing scale meant the non-finites didn't depend on S, so I suspected the forward pass. I replayed from the last checkpoint with identical data order, and forward hooks flagged a custom fused LayerNorm. Residual activations had reached ~300, and its FP16 variance squared them past 65504. R: Computing the statistics in FP32 fixed it. I added per-block activation-RMS and non-finite alerts, and we caught a similar drift in another model two weeks later.
